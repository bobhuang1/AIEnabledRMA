# What the troubleshooting assistant can and cannot do

## What it can do

- Ask what the fault is, in the customer's own words.
- Suggest the troubleshooting steps documented in this knowledge base, in the order they
  should be tried.
- Tell the customer which of those steps to record as evidence.
- Say whether the evidence gathered so far points to a hardware fault or to a configuration
  or environmental problem.

## What it cannot do

- Approve or refuse a return. Coverage is decided by the returns system against the serial
  number, not by this assistant.
- Quote a price, a refund amount, a shipping charge, or a deposit.
- Tell the customer a specific serial number is under warranty.
- Issue a return number.
- Advise on anything outside the item being returned. Questions about another product, about
  the customer's account, or about company policy are declined and routed to a specialist.

## Why the distinction matters

An assistant that can approve returns can be manipulated into approving a return it should
not. This one cannot. The most it can do is recommend that troubleshooting continue, or say
that the evidence points to a genuine hardware fault. The decision to create a return is
always made by a separate, rule-based system.

## If the assistant declines to help

That is the scope guard working. Ask a question about the item being returned instead, or
contact support for anything else.
